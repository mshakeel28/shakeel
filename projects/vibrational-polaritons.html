Vibrational polaritons and energy transfer

When a molecular vibration is strongly coupled to a cavity mode, the resulting polaritons open energy-transfer pathways that conventional molecular dynamics does not predict. Dual-frequency 2D IR lets us watch them.

Role
Measurement, analysis, device characterisation
Period
2024 – present
Techniques
Dual-frequency 2D IR, cavity dispersion measurement
Group
Rubtsov Lab, Tulane University

The question

Intramolecular vibrational redistribution normally follows the coupling structure of the molecule itself: energy leaves an excited mode along pathways set by anharmonic coupling to its neighbours. Placing that molecule inside an infrared photonic cavity mixes the vibration with the cavity field, and the question is whether the resulting polariton states carry energy in ways the bare molecule cannot.

Approach

Dual-frequency 2D IR excites one vibration and probes another, so energy arriving at a distant mode can be timed directly rather than inferred. Comparing the same molecule inside and outside the cavity isolates what the coupling changes.

I also characterised the microcavity structures themselves — measuring mode dispersion and coupling behaviour — and fed the spectroscopic requirements back to the collaborators who fabricated them as design constraints. That loop is what eventually pushed me into the cleanroom.

Results

This work contributed to Cohn et al., Controlling Intramolecular Vibrational Redistribution with an Infrared Photonic Cavity, published in The Journal of Physical Chemistry Letters in 2025, a collaboration between the Rubtsov group at Tulane and the Chuntonov group.
