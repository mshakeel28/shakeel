Nanofabrication for polariton optics

Vibrational strong coupling experiments need substrates whose optical resonance sits exactly on a molecular vibration. Rather than wait on collaborators for each new design, I am learning to make them in the cleanroom.

Role
Fabrication, process development
Period
2026 – present
Facility
Tulane Micro/Nanofabrication Facility
Tools
Suss MJB4 mask aligner, Angstrom e-beam evaporator

Why fabricate in house

Every strong-coupling measurement is bounded by the substrate. The resonance has to land on the vibrational mode of interest, the linewidth has to be narrow enough for the coupling to be resolvable, and the array has to survive sample handling. When substrates come from outside the group, each iteration costs weeks, which limits how many designs you can test.

Process

Aluminium microdisk arrays on CaF2, patterned by contact mask photolithography on the Suss MJB4 and metallised by electron-beam evaporation, followed by lift-off. CaF2 is the substrate of choice because it stays transparent through the mid-IR where the vibrations sit, but it is fragile and its surface chemistry is less forgiving than silicon or glass.
