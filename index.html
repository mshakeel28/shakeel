Following vibrational energy through molecules, membranes, and cavities.

PhD candidate in physical chemistry at Tulane University, using ultrafast dual-frequency 2D IR spectroscopy to measure how vibrational energy moves — and where it gets stuck.

My work spans surface-enhanced 2D IR of interfacial layers, polariton-mediated energy transfer under vibrational strong coupling, and antimicrobial peptide interactions with lipid bilayers probed by site-specific IR labels. I am now extending that work upstream into device fabrication, training in cleanroom photolithography and thin-film deposition so the group can produce its own plasmonic and photonic substrates.

Seven peer-reviewed publications, three years at the optical table, and a growing interest in roles where instrument design meets real measurement problems.

Research

2026 – present
Photolithography · E-beam evaporation

Nanofabrication for polariton optics

Fabricating aluminium microdisk surface-lattice-resonance arrays on CaF2 at Tulane's Micro/Nanofabrication Facility, bringing production of vibrational strong coupling substrates in house instead of depending on collaborators.

2024 – present
Dual-frequency 2D IR

Vibrational polaritons and energy transfer

Intramolecular vibrational relaxation under strong coupling, revealing polariton-mediated pathways beyond conventional molecular dynamics. Characterised collaborator-fabricated microcavities — mode dispersion, coupling behaviour — and translated spectroscopic requirements back into device design constraints.

2024 – present
FTIR · 2D IR · Site-specific labels

Antimicrobial peptides in lipid bilayers

Probing how the pore-forming peptide MelP5 perturbs POPC membranes across a temperature range, using site-specific IR labels to resolve structural and dynamical changes that ensemble measurements average away.

2022
SERS · PCA · PLS-DA

SERS for biofilm detection

Silver-nanoparticle SERS combined with chemometric classification to distinguish biofilm-forming bacterial strains of Staphylococcus epidermidis with high sensitivity and specificity.
